# **Do Quantum**: Initial Interest Activity

**Purpose**  
Practice technical reading and writing skills, while gaining exposure to a research area of your choice.
This should help you develop techniques to discover the truth, think and reason, and learn to support or reject ideas using evidence.

**Due**  
Wednesday, 23 September @ 11:59 pm

# Instructions

**Goal**
- Define and explore a question related to your industry or area of interest.
- Practice forming clear, testable claims, reasoning about their truth, and backing up your conclusions with credible sources.
- Communicate complex ideas, findings, and evidence simply and effectively. 

**Steps** (for each partner)
1. Partner up with 1 other student.
   It helps if you align with your partner on the industry or broad area of interest.
2. **Each** partner creates a layman’s hypothesis (simple, everyday language) related to the chosen area.
3. For each hypothesis, write a short paper (1-2 pages) with these sections:
   + Before: What do you think?
      1. Hypothesis: **one** clear sentence.
      2. Reasoning & Justification: why you think it might be true/false, using your background knowledge, logic, or intuition.
   + After: What did you find out?
      3. Research: what sources exist about it, and what do they say?
      4. Conclusion: whether you found out if your hypothesis is correct, isn't, or you can't tell.
      5. Bibliography: at least 3 credible sources (APA/MLA or similar).
   + Tips:  
     Don't edit the before section after you do research.  
     Intellectual integrity is important.  
     Even negative results & incorrect assumptions matter.   
     Real geniuses value simplicity.
4. Record a video (about 5 minutes per person) where you:  
   1. Present your topic, initial thoughts, and reasoning to your partner.  
   2. Walk them through your research and conclusions.  
   3. Discuss sources you found and why they matter.  
   4. Question & Answer section.
5. **ONCE ACCEPTED** Optionally, look at accepted teams to have a good idea of who else will be at the next meeting.

**Submission**
1. Navigate to the "Activity Folder."  
2. Create a folder for your partnership named: "industry: fun name you choose"
3. Upload your written document and video into that folder.  
4. Link your folder in the submission spreadsheet.
5. Be assessed as a team to know if you’re continuing by Thursday, 24 September @ 11:59 pm.

# Example

**Hypothesis** (before research)  
Students learn quantum concepts more easily if analogies from sports are used.

**Reasoning** (before research)  
Sports are familiar to most students, and analogies make abstract topics less intimidating.
If they understand the sports, then they can use the same logic to understand quantum.

**Research**
Research from UMD shows that analogies are effective in STEM education when the background material from the analogy is familiar to the students. More generally, older research shows that analogies are very useful in relating concepts, since they are building from a context that one may already be familiar with. This is used in practice in textbooks and classes.

**Conclusion** (after research)  
Likely true. Sources show analogical teaching helps retention, and sports examples reduce intimidation for beginners.

**Bibliography**
1. Gentner, D., & Colhoun, J. (2010). *Analogical processes in learning*. Cognitive Science.  
2. National Academies of Sciences. (2018). *How people learn II: Learners, contexts, and cultures*.  
3. University of Maryland Teaching and Learning Center. (2021). "Effective Use of Analogies in STEM Education."

# Rubric

**Hypothesis Development**
- Hypothesis is clearly stated, testable, and relevant.  
- Reasoning shows personal thought, not just copied text.

**Research & Evidence**
- At least 3 credible sources included.  
- Sources are cited in a consistent format.  
- Connections between evidence and conclusion are clear.

**Presentation Video**
- Both partners actively participate (5 minutes each).  
- Explanations are clear, logical, and engaging.  
- Discussion connects hypothesis, reasoning, and sources.

**Professionalism & Submission**
- Folder named correctly: "topic: fun name you choose."  
- Video and docs uploaded by deadline.  
- Organization, clarity, and adherence to instructions.

# Tips
1. Keep your hypothesis simple and relatable.  
2. Think about what evidence could prove or disprove it.  
3. Be honest: if your sources disagree with you, explain why you changed your mind.  
4. A strong bibliography shows depth, don’t rely only on blogs or Wikipedia.
5. LLMs (ChatGPT, DeepSeek, Claude, Grok) are nice for formatting and explanations, but you won't automatically develop an understanding from them.
