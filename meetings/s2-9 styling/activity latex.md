# **Do Quantum**: LaTeX Markup Activity

**Purpose**  
Practice reading and writing beautiful plots and figures, while learning about different techniques for visualizing data.
This should help you clearly communicate data visually, and identify the purpose of visualizations and understand the message they are communicating.

**Due**  
Wednesday, 30 September @ 11:59 pm

# Instructions

**Goal**
- Analyze and understand the purpose of visualizations in graph.
- Practice interpreting meaning and assessing utility in visualizations.
- Be able to create your own visualizations to effectively convey ideas.

**Setup**
Setting up a LaTeX environment is a very well-documented process. There are thousands of tutorials across the internet.
- If you will always have internet and want easy collaboration, Overleaf provides professional plans to university accounts.
- If you would like an offline setup, there are two components: the frontend, and the compiler. Recommendations:
  - [TeX Users Group](https://tug.org/texlive/) has the "TeX Live" compiler distribution.
  - [Texmaker](https://www.xm1math.net/texmaker/) is a free frontend for editing TeX and viewing the output. 

**Steps**
1. As a team, review your knowledge graphs and bibliography.
2. Review the diagrams, figures, tables, and equations in the paper.
   Pay attention to what was done well, what wasn't, and think about why.
3. Choose a few diagrams, charts, and plots that would suit your project well.  
   Take screenshots, and download the TeX source to the paper if it is provided.
4. Adapt the figures to visualize your data.
5. Ask another team to review. Does it make sense? Is the point clearly visualized?

**Submission**
1. Navigate to your team folder.
2. Make a subfolder for this activity labeled "LaTeX figures"  
2. Add your screenshots, code, and references.
