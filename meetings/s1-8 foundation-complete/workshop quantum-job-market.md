# **Do Quantum**: Quantum Job Market Workshop

**Purpose**
Help researchers understand how the skills, tools, and experiences they develop throughout the program connect to real opportunities in academia, industry, government, and entrepreneurship - and even leave with opportunities to apply to.
This workshop should help researchers identify opportunities they may want to pursue, understand the skills those opportunities require, and make more informed decisions about what they choose to learn and build throughout the remainder of the program.

**Duration**
25 Minutes

# Instructions

**Goal**
* Learn about opportunities currently available in quantum and adjacent fields.
* Understand how research, technical, communication, and leadership skills connect to future opportunities.
* Identify skills commonly requested by organizations hiring students and early-career professionals.
* Discover opportunities researchers may want to apply for now or prepare for later.
* Help researchers make informed decisions about which skills, tools, and projects they pursue throughout the remainder of the program.

**Steps**

1. Leadership will briefly present examples of opportunities across academia, government, public industry, and private industry. Examples may include:
    * Academic research positions.
    * Undergraduate research programs.
    * Graduate research opportunities.
    * Government laboratories.
    * National laboratories.
    * Large public technology companies.
    * Private quantum companies.
    * Startups.
    * Defense organizations.
    * Consulting firms.
    * Software and engineering positions that value technical research experience.
2. Identify Patterns Across Opportunities. Leadership will ask researchers:
    * Which skills appear repeatedly?
    * Which tools appear repeatedly?
    * Which experiences appear repeatedly?
    * Which opportunities seem most interesting?
    * Which opportunities seem least interesting?

For researcher positions, companies often look for people who fit a specific skillset or who have demonstrated an ability to understand new tools, problem, create solutions, and synthesize scientific ideas.

Researchers should begin identifying patterns between opportunities they find interesting and the skills those opportunities value.

Leadership should reinforce that the purpose of the program is not simply to complete projects, but to develop competence that translates to them creating value and capturing some percent as compensation - create value at their own company or someone elses.

3. Researchers will collaboratively search for opportunities they would realistically consider pursuing for the remainder of the workshop. Examples include:
    * Internships.
    * Research positions.
    * Fellowships.
    * Co-ops.
    * Graduate programs.
    * Startup opportunities.
    * Full-time positions.

# Success Criteria

**Opportunity Awareness**
* Researchers can identify opportunities they genuinely find interesting.
* Researchers understand that opportunities exist across multiple sectors.

**Skill Awareness**
* Researchers recognize skills employers and organizations consistently value.
* Researchers understand which skills they already possess and which they still need to develop.

**Program Alignment**
* Researchers can explain how their current work connects to future opportunities.
* Researchers can identify skills or tools they may want to prioritize throughout the remainder of the program.

**Action**
* Researchers identify opportunities they can apply for immediately.
* Researchers leave with a clearer idea of what they may want to pursue after the program.

# Tips
1. Focus on recurring skill requirements rather than any single job posting.
2. Demonstrated competence is usually more persuasive than claimed competence.
3. Research experience, communication skills, leadership experience, and technical skills frequently appear together.
4. The goal is not to choose a career today. The goal is to become more informed.
