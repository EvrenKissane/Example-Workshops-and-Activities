# **Do Quantum**: Hypothesis Creation Activity

**Purpose**  
Practice forming claims and breaking them down into their component factors.
This should help you identify variables and reason about how they interact as a system, so that you can form testable claims.

**Due**  
Wednesday, 14 October @ 11:59 pm

# Instructions

**Goal**
- Form and dissect a question in a research area of interest.
- Diagram a system as variables and propose cause & effect chains.
- Communicate a claim clearly and unambiguously.

**Steps**
1. In class, use a simple (not final) topic to follow the steps to find, narrow, and phrase your focus.
2. List out as many factors involved in the area as you can.
3. Draw lines between factors. On each arrow, label how the pair of factors are related.
   They can be correlated together/against, be cause & effect, be unrelated, or maybe there isn't a known relation.
   Find sources to justify connections where possible.
4. Assess your graph and think about which parts are weak or unsupported.
   Form a hypothesis to assert the relation on that edge, and think about how to test it.
5. Towards the end of the meeting, join with a partner to give a peer-review of the trial hypothesis development.
6. After class, before the next meeting, repeat these steps more thoroughly using a topic of your choice.

**Submission**
1. Navigate to the "Activity Folder."  
2. Create a folder with your name.
3. Upload your diagram(s) into that folder, accompanying written explanations, and any reference materials.  
4. Link your folder in the submission spreadsheet.
5. Get feedback from peers, peer advisors, and leadership by the next meeting.

# Checklist

**Focus**
- Research focus is clearly defined and reasonably narrow.
- Question and topic is coherent.
- Factors covered in graph connect to the central theme or idea.

**Graph**
- At least four relevant variables are identified.
- Variable relationships form a fully connected graph.
- Relationships are connected with explanations, including reasoning and sources.

**Research & Evidence**
- Connections between references and diagrams are clear.
- Sources are included as documents and/or can be traced to the origin.

**Submission**
- Files and versions named clearly.
- Materials uploaded by deadline.
- Organization, clarity, and adherence to instructions.

# Tips
1. LLMs can search for mentions of variable relations, but make sure there is real evidence before you believe the LLM output.
2. Think about why and how variables are related on your own too, so you develop an intuition for identifying potential connections.
3. Some nice flowchart software: Graphviz, Mermaid.js, Lucidchart
4. Don't worry about the "wow" factor in a claim. First, work on the structure. Emphasizing the "wow" is a separate skill.
