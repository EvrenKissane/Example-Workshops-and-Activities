# Hows
1. Structuring: What is involved in the design of a test?
   - Variables:
     X: Independent - This variable is what you would control.
     Y: Dependent   - This is a variable should change when the independent variable changes.
     C: Control     - This variable should have no effect on the relationship between X and Y.
     B: Constant    - This variable exists and cannot be changed.

2. Planning: How can I actually do the test?
   - Resource allocation:
     Who will do what? Do you need outside expertise?
     How will it be done? Are you using a quantum computer, simulators, physics labs, or paper and pencil?
     What resources do you need? What do you have access to?
   - Historical:
     What have other people done similarly? What haven't people done?
     What hasn't worked before? What has?

3. Testing: How do I restate the claim as a conclusion?
   - Logical reasoning patterns
     Deductive: General premises derive a specific conclusion. P and Q means R
     Inductive: Specific premises derive a general conclusion. A1 and A2 and ... and An means An+1
     Abductive: Recovering a specific conclusion from incomplete premises. P meant Q before, so Q is evidence of P.
     Conductive / Analogical: Identifying patterns to predict a conclusion. A means Q and R, B means Q and R, so if C means Q then it might mean R too.
   - Probability effects logical reasoning. See confidence intervals and p-values.
