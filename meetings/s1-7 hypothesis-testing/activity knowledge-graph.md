# **Do Quantum**: Hypothesis Testing Activity

**Purpose**  
Practice tracing research back to the source and explore real papers in the field.
This should help you gain exposure to a variety of information and help you create a bibliography.

**Due**  
Wednesday, 21 October @ 11:59 pm

# Instructions

**Goal**
- Scan through papers for citations to form a network.
- Analyze the purpose of citations and how they connect work.
- Notice high-level trends, dependencies, and patterns across publications.

**Steps**
1. With an initial first paper, identify 3-8 citations.  
   Consider the balance of research directions; if a quantum algorithms paper cites both mathematics and physics papers, explore both paths.
2. Find what each citation justifies or brings to the paper, and write that on an arrow. Each arrow should go from the citation to the paper that cites it.
   Keep track of where each paper came from and where it is now located. Notice when papers are in the same lab, journal, website, company, etc..
3. Take each new paper in the graph and repeat until you have approximately 30 papers in the graph.
   You can also look for papers that cite the ones you are looking at to go forwards in time on your graph.
4. Write a 1-2 paragraphs in a separate document to assess any high-level patterns across the knowledge graph you constructed.
   Did a question, error, or methodology propagate unchanged?
   Did a specific person make a chain of research papers, each following up on each other?

**Submission**
1. Navigate to the "Activity Folder."  
2. Create a folder with your name.
3. Upload your diagram(s) into that folder, accompanying written explanations, and any reference materials.  
4. Link your folder in the submission spreadsheet.
5. Get feedback from peers, peer advisors, and leadership by the next meeting.

# Rubric

**Coherence**
- Knowledge graph is fully connected.
- Selected papers are on-topic, and if off-topic papers show up, justification is provided.

**Connections**
- Citations are explained clearly and match their purpose in the paper.
- Reasonable effort was taken to explore each topic that was cited by the paper.

**References**
- Publications are included, or easy to follow back to their original source: DOI, ISBN, PMID, ORCID, OCLC, ...
- At least 30 papers were assessed.

**Submission**
- Files and versions named clearly.  
- Materials uploaded by deadline.  
- Organization, clarity, and adherence to instructions.  

# Tips
1. LLMs are nice for summarizing the purpose of citations, but sometimes they are nuanced and need careful review.
2. There are many reasons to cite a paper beyond just content. Methodology, formatting, magic numbers, or even an evil review committee.
3. Some nice flowchart software: Graphviz, Mermaid.js, Lucidchart
