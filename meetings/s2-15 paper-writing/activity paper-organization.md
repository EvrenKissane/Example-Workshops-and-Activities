# **Do Quantum**: Paper Organization Activity

**Purpose**  
Practice analyzing the structure of research publications.
This should help you identify structure in papers, so that you can quickly find and extract relevant material.

**Due**  
Wednesday, 10 February @ 11:59 pm

# Instructions

**Goal**
- Organize thoughts into clear and coherent sections in your writing.
- Recognize the organization and intentions of sections in others' writing.
- Quickly find, spot and interpret relevant material from papers.

**Steps**
1. Find a paper that you are familiar with.
2. Extract an outline of the document.
   Include top-level sections: abstract, introduction, methods, results, conclusion, references, etc.
   You may also include second-level sections.
3. Measure the length of each section: a few sentences, several paragraphs, many pages, ...
   You should have a dictionary that maps section names from step 2 to their respective length.
4. Consider the point of the paper. What is it primarily bringing into the world?
   Is it a: methods paper, experimental design, validation, algorithm, or something else?
5. Repeat from step 1 using another paper until you have statistics for at 10-15 papers.
6. Group the length maps from step 3 together based on what you determined was the point of the paper in step 4.
   Do you notice any patterns? Are they similar, two groups, or are there outliers?
   Longer sections can indicate more effort, but that is not always the case.

**Submission**
1. Navigate to your team folder.
2. Create a sheet with tables of data from step 2 and 3, and a document with your interpretation.
3. Get feedback from peers, peer advisors, and leadership by the next meeting.

# Rubric

**Clarity**
- Top-level sections are general enough to appear in a majority of documents.
- Organizational structure created to count section length contain quantifiable and comparable data.
- Sections for the point of each paper make sense and are not topic-specific.

**Analysis**
- Charts, tables, or spreadsheets that visualize the statistics collected
- Enough data was recorded to make a meaningful analysis.

**Interpretation**
- Outliers, patterns, or lack thereof are addressed and interpreted.
- Don't focus on the topic, but the type of the paper.

**Submission**
- Files and versions named clearly.
- Materials uploaded by deadline.
- Organization, clarity, and adherence to instructions.

# Tips
1. LLMs are historically bad at counting. But they can identify complexity in writing.
2. Outliers might not signal that your classification was incorrect; you might have just found a bad paper.
3. This is a chance to learn how to analyze data with spreadsheets or programming in R, Julia, Matlab, or Python.
